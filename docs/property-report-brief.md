## The property report brief (summary of the owner's pasted spec)
Do not rebuild; review the existing Homes report first, keep what works, keep Simple/Advanced.
- **Top of the report, in plain words:** net wealth at the end for buying and for the alternative
  the user chose, the difference, the years, the main assumptions, and that it could change.
  No "Buying is ahead" shorthand; do not force a winner when close. Keep apart: does the property
  gain value / is buying better than the alternative / is it affordable and resilient for them /
  non-financial reasons.
- **Main graph "How could your financial position change over time?":** both options' net wealth
  (or a clearly labelled difference with a zero line), the central estimate, the scenario range,
  crossovers only where the calculation finds them, the holding period and end values. Fair
  comparison: same start and horizon, no double counting.
- **Scenario range (core):** favourable / central / unfavourable, each RECALCULATED THROUGH THE
  REAL MODEL (never a band drawn around the line - the owner stressed this), shaded band, zero
  line, break-even marks. Sliders (property growth, investment return, bond rate, rent rise,
  maintenance inflation, unexpected repairs...) with central and bounds editable; few in Simple,
  more in Advanced. Explain what the range is and is not (a sensitivity range, not a confidence
  interval); interpret it from the actual results (all one side / crosses zero / wide / narrow);
  say which assumptions must go right or wrong to change the answer; document which combinations
  are used.
- **Other graphs:** monthly housing cost by part (bond, running, maintenance, rent), bond paid off
  is not cost-free; upfront vs recurring costs; "What moves the answer most" ranked and explained;
  year-by-year table, collapsible, agreeing with the graphs.
- **Personal risk assessment:** the decision's own risk (cash up front, cost vs income, reserves
  left, rate rise, repairs, low growth, holding period, optimism) and personal resilience
  (optional: stage of life, years to retirement/goal, dependants, income stability, debts,
  emergency savings, big expenses, comfort with uncertainty). Risk capacity vs risk tolerance kept
  apart. No invented scores; unknowns shown as not assessed; explain the factors; options to
  improve resilience, not instructions.
- **Why each input and result matters:** short explanations, expandable.
- **"Did you think of…":** context-sensitive; entered / estimated / unknown / not applicable kept
  apart; never treat an unknown as zero silently.
- **Order:** summary, net-wealth graph with range, key reasons, monthly cash flow, costs, risk,
  assumptions and checklist, sensitivity controls and year by year.
- Not financial advice, no guarantees; honest about what is known, estimated and uncertain.
- Acceptance criteria: the 15 in the owner's brief (plain comparison first, over time, consistent,
  real scenario range, explained, drivers shown, resilience not age alone, missing info visible,
  explanations, modes kept, mobile readable, nothing broken, not advice, helps understand
  trade-offs).
