# Homes: ten reasons for buying - how each one differs (plan, 2026-10-30)

**Parked (2026-10-30).** The owner wants the current report (Buying, Ours already, Renting) right
first - its graphs and what they answer. Come back to this afterwards. Nothing here is built.

## What every reason shares

- **At the top:** "Why are you buying?" (the ten, one per home), then **Paying with** (bond and
  deposit / cash), **The differences** (invested / kept as cash), and **How long** (3, 5, 7, 10, 15
  years, until the bond is paid off, or your own number).
- **A summary line** naming what is analysed, e.g. "Buying Marisa Park to move into, instead of
  renting at R14 000 a month - differences invested - over 15 years".
- **Your figures** (the input sections) fold into one line once there is an answer; only the
  sections the reason needs are shown, and hidden ones keep what was typed.
- **Three graphs:**
  1. the comparison with its range;
  2. monthly costs by part;
  3. value, bond and equity.
- **Explore how the result could change** under the main graph:
  - Lower · Your estimate · Upper as numbers, one note per assumption, and Narrow / Wider / Custom.
  - A lower value can never be above your estimate, nor an upper value below it.
  - Changing one redraws the graph, the answer, the crossing and the ranking.
- **What makes the biggest difference:** your value, lower and upper, the rand effect, and whether
  it can turn the answer, with Adjust beside each.
- **Can you carry it?**, **Did you think of…** and **Year by year** follow.
- **"Ahead" never stands alone:** every result names both sides.
- **The same money, always:** both options start with the same savings and have the same money each
  month. Whoever needs less invests the difference (or keeps it as cash). At the end, each is what
  it would leave you: homes sold after selling costs, bonds and tax, plus everything invested.
- **One investment return** (the two fields merge).
- **A month already past** is counted from then, with today marked.

## The ten

You pay two lots of housing until it is done. The value when finished. |

## Looking back: was it the right call? (the home we own, or the one we rent)

Not a reason for buying, so it is not one of the ten. It is what an "Ours already" or a "Renting"
page does by default:
- **The top row there:** "Check how it has done" (the default) / "Compare it with something"
  (reason 4).
- **"Right" needs something to be right against**, so the page builds it from one or two figures,
  without comparing it with a saved home.

| Page | The question | What it is checked against | Extra inputs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ours already | Was buying it the right call, and from when? | Renting a similar home from the month we bought, and investing the deposit, buying costs and every month's difference | What a similar home rents for now (or rented for then); rent rises |
| Renting | Was renting the right call, and from when? | Buying a similar home in the month we started renting, with the deposit we had then | What a similar home sold for then, the bond rate then, the deposit we had |

- **The answer:**
  - so far (today);
  - from when it became the better choice (the crossing, if there is one);
  - and, if you stay as long as you plan, where it would leave you then.
- **Tracking:** your real figures make it truer over time.
  - The bond's history is already logged (rate changes, paying more, taking out).
  - What the home is worth now can be updated whenever you hear a value.
  - Rent increases can be logged the same way, as a list of "from this month, this rent".
- **No market figures are invented.** A similar home's rent or price comes from you, e.g. from
  listings. Until you give one, the page shows only what it can state on its own and asks for it.
  What it can state on its own:
  - what the home has grown by a year, against inflation;
  - the equity built;
  - what it has cost, item by item.

Built in round 1, with reasons 1 and 2: the same engine, run from the start month.

## The range per reason

- 1, 2, 5, 6: house prices, investments, the bond rate, rent rises (2, 5, 6), running costs, repairs.
- 3, 7, 8: those, plus the rent level (±10%) and months empty (0 to 3).
- 9: renovation overrun, the selling price, months to sell, the bond rate.
- 10: building overrun, months to build, house prices, the bond rate.
- Repairs are tested only when there is a maintenance figure; otherwise the page says so.

## Can you carry it, per reason

- **All:** the month against income, a 2-point rate rise, cash left after day one, and a big repair.
- **1:** our home taking long to sell (bridging, two bonds).
- **3, 5, 6, 7, 8:** three months without rent (empty, or a tenant not paying) in rand.
- **9, 10:** going over budget and over time, and the cash to finish.

## Defaults to label as illustrative (no invented market figures)

- Months empty: 1 a year.
- Letting agent: 10% plus VAT.
- Student holidays: 2 months empty.
- Renovation contingency: 15%.
- Building contingency: 10%.

Each says it is a starting point for the owner to replace, not market data.
